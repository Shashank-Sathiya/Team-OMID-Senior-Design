# OMID Project Constraints Essay

## Economic (Manufacturability) 

Project OMID operates under a strict prototype development budget of $1,500 allocated for acquiring transparent organic photovoltaic (OPV) materials and testing microcontrollers. Because custom molecular synthesis is cost-prohibitive for undergraduate teams, we must rely on commercial off-the shelf semi-transparent films rather than expensive custom fabrication. 

## Environmental (Sustainability) 

Unlike traditional opaque silicon panels, our deployment targets regional Midwest family farms cultivating photosensitive crops that require continuous visible light transmission. The system architecture completely avoids toxic heavy metals, ensuring that weathered or decommissioned panels pose zero chemical leaching hazards to agricultural topsoil. Furthermore, utilizing non-visible infrared and ultraviolet spectra reduces the environmental footprint of auxiliary farm machinery power generation. 

## Security 

To safeguard proprietary crop energy yields and telemetry transmitted from field nodes, the system implements AES-128 encryption across all wireless communication links. Unsecured local network protocols are strictly prohibited to prevent malicious eavesdropping on operational greenhouse energy statistics. 

## Legal 

Commercial deployment must comply with IEEE 1547 standards governing distributed energy resources interconnecting with local agricultural microgrids. Intellectual property agreements with university research labs restrict open-source redistribution of our specific transparent OPV integration firmware. Finally, this security implementation directly conflicts with our economic constraint, requiring us to balance low-cost microcontrollers against the high processing overhead of robust cryptographic protocols. Meeting these overlapping legal and security mandates ensures our system remains viable for commercial agricultural testing without risking compliance penalties.
